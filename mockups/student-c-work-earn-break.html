    What staff do

    The whole plan is three rules. If every adult in the room runs them the same way, the board does the teaching and nobody has to negotiate.

    1  Work the strip

    He pulls the top card off the strip, does that activity, then Velcros the card into the Done pocket at the bottom. The next card down becomes the top card.

    Hand-over-hand the card to Done at first if he walks away from it. Moving the card is part of the routine, not an extra step.

    2  Earn the break

    When he reaches a green star, he has earned it. He takes the Chromebook card off the top strip, carries it to the device desk, and it stays there while the timer runs.

    SayYou earned it. Chromebook.

    When the timer goes off, the Chromebook card goes back up on the top strip and he returns to the work desk. Putting the card back is what ends the break — not an adult closing the laptop.

    3  Two cards come off

    Yelling takes down Calm voice. Unsafe hands takes down Calm hands. Either one also takes down the Chromebook — every time, no exceptions.

    Say — flat, onceNo calm voice. No Chromebook.

    Then stop talking. Take both cards down where he can see it happen and hold them. No explaining, no warnings, no counting.

    Giving the cards back

    As soon as he is calm, hand him the Calm voice card and let him put it back up. Then hand him the Chromebook card and let him put that up too.

    SayCalm voice. Put it up.  ·  Chromebook back.

    He always re-Velcros the cards himself. That is the part that teaches the link. Then go back to the same schedule card he was on.

    Prep list

    • Laminated cards: calm voice, calm hands, Chromebook, task box, sorting, math, clean up, and two green stars
    • Velcro dots — hook on the board, loop on the cards, so it is the same everywhere
    • A Done pocket or envelope at the bottom of the board
    • A visual countdown timer at the device desk
    • A duplicate set in the go-bag for specials and related services

    What we track

    Tally two things per session: how many cycles he finishes, and how many card removals happen. That is the whole data sheet.

    If removals climb, the work is probably running too long between stars rather than the plan failing. Shorten the run before changing anything else.

    Decisions the team still needs to make

    The mockup had to pick an answer for each of these so it could run. None of them are settled — bring an opinion.

    How long is the break?
    The demo uses a short timer so it fits inside a meeting. Mockup assumes: 5 minutes in the room.
    What if he yells during Chromebook time?
    Right now the break ends and the card comes off. The alternative is pausing the timer and giving the remaining minutes back once he is calm. Mockup assumes: the break is over.
    How calm, for how long, before cards go back?
    Immediately, or after a set quiet interval? A fixed number is easier to run consistently across staff. Mockup assumes: staff judgment, returned right away.
    Where do removed cards go?
    Held in an adult’s hand where he can see them, or into a pocket on the board? Mockup assumes: staff holds them in view.
    What counts as “hands not safe”?
    This needs one written definition every adult uses, or the rule gets applied differently by different people and stops meaning anything. Mockup assumes: not yet defined.
    Does this ride along to specials?
    Same board and same rules in art, music, and PE, or work area only? Mockup assumes: work area only.
